\# Chart Choice



\## 1. Which month has the highest total revenue?



\*\*May\*\* has the highest total revenue among April, May, and June.



For a monthly revenue comparison, a \*\*bar chart\*\* is appropriate because the months are discrete categories and the goal is to compare their total revenue.



The message should be clear within 10 seconds. The y-axis should start at zero so the visual comparison is not misleading.



\---



\## 2. Ethnic Wear share of April total revenue



Ethnic Wear contributes \*\*24.92%\*\* of April's total revenue.



For showing the share of Ethnic Wear within April's total, a \*\*part-to-whole chart\*\* can be used because the question focuses on one category's contribution to one month's total.



This is a simple univariate composition view and should communicate the share clearly within 10 seconds.



\---



\## 3. Compare the four regions



The four regions can be compared using a \*\*bar chart\*\* with region on one axis and total revenue on the other.



This is a categorical comparison and therefore fits a \*\*univariate\*\* view of regional revenue.



The regional values are:



\- North: INR 337125.46

\- West: INR 333106.33

\- South: INR 316736.68

\- East: INR 275098.45



The chart should make the differences between the four regions clear within 10 seconds.



If revenue is compared with another variable such as order count, a \*\*bivariate\*\* view can be used. If several dimensions are analysed together, such as region, revenue, order count, and category, a \*\*multivariate\*\* view may be appropriate.



\---



\## Chart Design Rules



\- The message should be understandable within 10 seconds.

\- The y-axis should start at zero for revenue comparisons.

\- Avoid 3D charts because they can make magnitude comparisons harder.

\- Use a legend only when multiple series require one.

\- Keep the chart simple and focused on the business question.
